What Chronic Kidney Disease in Large Breeds Really Means (And Why It's Not 'Kidney Failure')

July 31, 2026 · 5 min read · PawWell Editorial

The moment your giant pup drinks the whole bowl again

Youve just come in from a gentle beach walk a bit of breeze, a few pats from strangers who always say hes like a small horse, isnt he? and your old Labrador or Rottie drains the water bowl like its a race. Then he asks for more. Your mind ticks over. Is this just the salt air? A warm afternoon? Or that word youve been dreading kidney disease?

If youve seen a blood test result come back with creatinine up or SDMA borderline, I want you to pause right here, cuppa in hand. Because theres a huge difference between a lab slip with a little asterisk and the end of the road. Chronic kidney disease (CKD) isnt the same as sudden kidney failure, and it certainly doesnt mean youve run out of options. In fact, most large-breed dogs diagnosed early are in a position many owners dont expect one where a few thoughtful changes can keep that tail wagging for a long time yet.

The short answer: abnormal kidney values kidney failure

Chronic kidney disease is a long-term, slowly progressive condition a bit like the way a well-loved verandah weathers over years, not a storm that knocks it flat overnight. Vets stage it from 1 to 4 using guidelines from the International Renal Interest Society (IRIS). Stage 1 is the earliest, often with no outward symptoms at all, just a persistently dilute urine or a slightly elevated SDMA on a blood test. Stage 4 is where the kidneys are working much less, and signs like vomiting or poor appetite become more obvious. Between those two points is a whole lot of living.

So when you hear chronic kidney disease, think manageable condition, not terminal illness. Even dogs with stage 2 or 3 CKD can enjoy their daily sniffari, slow ambles, and cheese bribes for quite a while especially with the right support from your vet.

Illustrative scenario not a real patient

Bear is an 8-year-old Golden Retriever from the Sunshine Coast. During a routine senior check-up, his bloodwork showed creatinine just a touch above normal and an SDMA of 18 g/dL. His owner panicked shed once lost a family dog to kidney failure and thought this was the same. But Bear was bright, eating well, and his urine test showed mild protein loss. His vet explained he was IRIS stage 2, not kidney failure. With a renal-support diet, fresh water always available, and check-ups every three months, Bear stayed happy and snoozing by the door for two more years before gradually moving to stage 3. His owner now says, I wish Id known earlier that kidney disease wasnt the same as goodbye.

Whats actually happening inside those kidneys? (One thing to understand)

Kidneys dont just make wee they filter waste, keep minerals balanced, help control blood pressure, and tell the body to make red blood cells. In CKD, the tiny filter units called nephrons slowly get damaged and cant be repaired. But the body is clever: the remaining healthy nephrons work a bit harder to compensate. Thats why a dog can lose quite a bit of kidney function before blood values like creatinine even budge.

SDMA is a newer marker that can give vets an earlier heads-up research shows it may rise when about 2540% of kidney function is lost, compared to creatinine only rising at roughly 75% loss. This doesnt mean you should panic at a single abnormal result. It means your vet now has better tools to spot a trend and start simple measures before your dog feels poorly. Staging only happens once CKD is confirmed, so a one-off high creatinine from dehydration or a hard romp is not a stage and definitely not a death sentence.

Today you can do this

  • Take a deep breath. An abnormal kidney value on a blood test is a signal to investigate, not a reason to despair.
  • Note any changes in your dogs drinking, peeing, appetite, or energy over the last few weeks even little ones. Jot them down for your vet.
  • Check if your dogs water bowl is easy to reach, especially on warm nights. Large breeds with creaky joints sometimes dont drink enough because the bowls in another room.
  • Ask your vet whether a follow-up urinalysis or blood pressure check would be useful these help complete the picture for accurate staging.

At-home self-check checklist: what you can watch for tonight

Grab a torch and have a quiet look while your dog is snoozing. No need to turn your home into a vet clinic, just a few mindful observations:

  • Water intake: Is it much more than usual, or has it dropped? Marking the water bowl level in the morning can help.
  • Urine output and colour: Larger puddles on the lawn? Very pale or clear urine may mean the kidneys arent concentrating properly.
  • Appetite: Is your dog still excited for dinner, or has he started turning away from foods he normally loves?
  • Weight and body condition: Can you still feel his ribs with a light touch? Unexpected weight loss is worth mentioning.
  • Mouth and breath: A whiff of ammonia or an unusual smell? Red, sore gums might signal build-up of waste products.
  • General demeanour: Is he still keen for a gentle walk, or unusually flat? Limping or stiff movement may suggest hidden discomfort from high blood pressure or uremic toxins.

When to call your vet right away

Urgent warning: if your dog keeps vomiting, completely refuses water or food, suddenly cannot urinate or urine drops sharply, becomes extremely weak, collapses, has seizures, shows confused or abnormal consciousness, or may have eaten grapes, raisins, human medicines, antifreeze or other toxins, contact an emergency vet immediately. Do not wait for symptoms to ease, and do not induce vomiting, force water, give fluids at home, or use human medicines. If poisoning is suspected, keep the packaging, note the time and estimated amount, and call the vet.

Gentle words to carry with you

I know that hearing the word kidney from your vet can make your stomach drop. You love that enormous, hairy mate of yours, and the thought of anything taking him early is heavy. But please know this: abnormal kidney values give you a chance to act, not a reason to give up. Many large-breed dogs live years with stable CKD still demolishing their dinner, still wagging their ridiculous tails when you walk in the room. Your job isnt to fix the kidneys; its to partner with your vet, give your dog the good stuff he needs, and soak up all the slobbery kisses you can.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is drinking more water always a sign of kidney disease?
Not at all. Increased thirst can come from simple things like hot weather, a salty treat, or even medications like cortisone. But if your large breed is suddenly draining the bowl every few hours and it persists for more than a couple of days, its worth a vet check. The key is pattern, not a single thirsty afternoon.

Can a dog with CKD eat a normal diet?
Most dogs with diagnosed CKD benefit from a diet with controlled levels of high-quality protein, phosphorus, and omega-3s. This isnt about no meat its about the right balance so the kidneys arent overloaded with waste. Your vet will guide you on whether a therapeutic renal diet is appropriate. Please dont change food suddenly or add supplements without a vets okay, as some well-meaning additions can strain kidneys further.

How long can a dog live with chronic kidney disease?
Theres no one-size-fits-all answer, because lifespan depends on the stage at diagnosis, your dogs overall health, and how well you can support him. Many dogs in IRIS stages 1 and 2 live comfortably for years. The goal isnt a number its maintaining tail wags, good appetite, and peaceful days for as long as possible.
