Kidney Health

Large Dog Peeing Dark or Bloody? What It Might Mean (and What to Do Tonight)

August 01, 2026 · 6 min read · PawWell Editorial

Youre on the back lawn, waiting for Buster to finish his morning wee so you can both go in for brekkie. Hes a big boy, a ten-year-old Golden Retriever, so youre used to a decent puddle. But this morning, you notice something that makes you stop mid-yawn. The wee is darker than usualdeep amber, maybe a rusty colour. You crouch down, and your heart sinks a little when you spot a definite pinkish tinge in the grass. What does that mean?

Dark urine or blood in a big dogs wee always warrants attention, but the cause can range from a simple, easily-fixed bladder infection to something that needs faster treatment. The most important first step is calm observation. In many cases, its a treatable urinary tract issue, but because large breeds can be prone to certain kidney problems, we do need to rule those out. You won't fix this just with extra water tonight, and yes, a vet visit is very likely on the cards, but you can gather some brilliant clues right now to help your mate.

Illustrative scenario - not a real patient.

Wendy, a mum of two Rottweilers, rang me in a flap last week. Her girl, Isla, had pee the colour of weak cordial and was squatting more often. Wendy thought it was just a UTI, but also mentioned Isla had been drinking the backyard pond dry. A quick vet check found not an infection, but the early stages of a bladder stone. Because they caught it early from just looking closely, Isla was treated and back to perfect health. It wasn't a disaster, just a clue.

What Dark or Bloody Urine Can Point To

In senior large breeds, we generally think of a few common culprits. Blood in the urineyour vet will call it haematuriasimply means red blood cells are getting into the pee somewhere from the kidneys down to the urethra.

A urinary tract infection (UTI) is often the first thought, and yes, its common. Bacteria irritate the bladder lining, causing it to bleed. You might also see your dog straining to wee, licking their bits more, or having small accidents inside. Its uncomfortable but usually sorts quickly with a vets antibiotic.

Then there are bladder or kidney stones. These are hard mineral deposits that can scrape the delicate lining of the urinary tract, like sandpaper. Large breeds like Dalmatians, though crossed with many of our goldies and labs, can be prone to specific stones. The wee might be dark, sometimes just at the end of the stream.

Sadly, for giant senior dogsBernese Mountain Dogs, Mastiffswe must consider kidney disease. The kidneys are incredible filters, and when theyre struggling, blood or protein can leak into the urine. Often, youll notice your mate is also drinking much more, maybe off his food, and perhaps having a few vomits. This is why the 'dark urine' alarm bell is one we take seriously. Less common, but always on our minds, is a mass or growth in the bladder or prostate. This isn't the most likely cause, but it is one your vet will want to rule out, especially if your big fella or girl is desexed later in life or has recurring issues.

Today you can do: A Calm, Simple Home Check

Before you ring the vet, try to note down a few things. Dont obsess, just be curious. This will help the vet enormously and make you feel like youre already helping your mate.

  • Catch a fresh sample if you can. Slide a clean, shallow takeaway container under them mid-stream (dont worry, weve all been there, balancing it on a broom handle!). This is gold for the vet.
  • Check the colour chart. Is it pale pink, like watered-down raspberry cordial? Bright red? Cola brown? The shade can give clues. Write it down in your phone.
  • Watch their style. Are they squatting or lifting but straining for ages? Only getting a few drops out? This is a classic sign of urgency or blockage.
  • Check the water bowl and mood. Is the bowl being drained faster than usual? Is your normally happy-go-lucky goof suddenly quiet or, conversely, restless and can't settle?
  • Gently feel their tummy. While theyre standing, run your hands softly along their belly and under their back. A tight, rock-hard abdomen that they flinch from is a clear signal. Don't push hard.

When to call your vet right away

Dont wait for tomorrow if you see these signs. This isn't about being panicked; it's about being a sensible, loving owner who knows when things move from a niggle to a genuine emergency.

Danger-Signal Alert:
If your dog stops urinating entirely but is clearly trying and uncomfortable, thats a straight-to-the-vet signal. A blocked urethra is life-threatening. Also, if you see a swollen, painful belly, pale or grey gums, repeated vomiting, or your big mate suddenly seems unable to stand or is collapsing, do not wait contact your vet or an emergency vet clinic immediately. These signs can indicate acute kidney injury which can be life-threatening within hours.

What the vet will probably do (so you can breathe easy)

When you take your mate in, the vets first job is to work out if this is a bladder problem (lower urinary tract) or a kidney problem (upper urinary tract). Theyll likely start with a proper urine analysis. That little sample you caught? It gets spun down in a machine to look for red cells, white cells, crystals (which point to stones), and protein.

Blood tests often follow, particularly for our senior large breeds, to check those kidney values (creatinine and SDMA are the big ones) and make sure everything else is humming along. If they suspect stones, imaging like an ultrasound or x-ray can give a clear picture. A sweet tip: an ultrasound is painless and often you can see the screen and the vet will show you whats going on. It can be terrifying sitting in the waiting room, but knowing this gentle process helps.

A quiet word on supplements and diet

If youre desperately Googling and seeing cranberry supplements or apple cider vinegar, pause for a moment. While some urinary diets work wonders, theyre prescription-only for a reasonthey change the pH of the urine. Giving the wrong supplement for the wrong type of stone can actually make them grow faster. Please dont waste your money or risk making things worse without having your vet identify whats truly going on. With a clear diagnosis, diet becomes a powerful tool, especially prescription foods designed to support failing kidneys. Seeing blood in your big mates wee is a shock. Its perfectly normal to feel a jolt of worry that sits in your stomach all day. But youre doing the right thing by learning. For every frightening story, there are hundreds of dogs who pee a bit pink, get a quick course of antibiotics, and are back to chasing the frisbee on the oval in a week. Trust your gut, observe your dogs daily rhythm, and never, ever feel silly for heading to the vet. You know your dog better than any Google search does.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can my dog's dark urine just be from dehydration after a hot Aussie walk?
Dehydration makes urine very concentrated and dark yellow, but it won't make it pink or red. If you see a red tinge, rusty colour, or definite blood clots, that's not just from not drinking enough. Encourage them to drink, by all means, but still plan for a vet check to rule out blood.

My old Labrador is peeing blood but seems completely finestill eating and wagging. Do I still need a vet?
Yes, absolutely. Dogs are masters at hiding discomfort, and the presence of blood means there is a physical problem that needs to be identified. A UTI might not make them sick systemically at first, but it can track up to the kidneys if ignored. Even if they seem bright as a button, get that urine sample checked within 24 hours.

How is a urine sample collected from a big, heavy dog that's hard to lift?
Youre not alone in dreading this! You dont need to lift them. The best method is the 'clean catch': attach a clean plastic ladle to a long ruler with tape, and slip it under them mid-stream before emptying it into a clean jar. For very large or touch-sensitive dogs, many vets use a technique called cystocentesis (a quick needle into the bladder guided by ultrasound) to get a perfectly sterile sample without any stress for the dog.
