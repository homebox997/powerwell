When Your Senior Dog Shows No Pain: Why Stoicism Doesn't Mean Comfort

August 10, 2026 · 6 min read · PawWell Editorial

One of the most common myths I hear from loving Aussie dog mums and dads is this: If hes not yelping or whining, he cant be in real pain. It makes sense, doesnt it? We cry out when we hurt, so surely a dog would tooespecially a big, expressive Lab or a gentle giant like a Bernese Mountain Dog. But heres the quiet truth: dogs are masters at hiding discomfort, and large senior breeds often suffer in silence long before youd ever see them so much as limp.

Picture this: youre having your morning cuppa on the back deck, and old Blue, your 10-year-old Rottie, slowly heaves himself off his bed. He still gives you that full-body tail wag, but youve noticed he doesnt jump up onto the ute anymore, and hell happily walk on the flat but avoids the back steps. He hasnt made a sound. Thats exactly how hidden pain often looksa gradual fading of the things he used to love, without a single whimper. This guide will walk you through what silent pain looks like, why big dogs are so stoic, and what you can do tonight to offer real comfort.

Bellas tail wags, but her hips ache

Illustrative scenario not a real patient. Bella is a 9-year-old Golden Retriever whos been the heartbeat of a young family in Brisbane. She still greets everyone at the door with a sock in her mouth, but her owners noticed shed stopped jumping onto the kids trampoline mat to snooze. At the beach, shed paddle in the shallows but refused to chase the ball into deeper water. Her appetite and tail-wag were unchanged.

When her vet gently examined her hips and lower spine, Bellas muscles tensed just slightlyenough to tell a story. X-rays showed moderate hip arthritis that had been quietly building for years. The family was stunned; Bella had never cried. But once she started a pain-management plan (weight control, joint supplements, and carefully chosen pain relief from her vet), within weeks she was wading chest-deep at the beach again and stealing socks with renewed vigour.

Why dogs hide pain: a survival instinct that can fool us

In the wild, an animal that shows weakness gets targeted by predators or loses its place in the pack. Dogs carry that same hardwired instinct thousands of years later. Large breedsthink Mastiffs, German Shepherds, and Retrieversare particularly skilled at masking discomfort. Its not stubbornness; its an ancient script that says keep going, stay strong. Chronic pain, like the slow grind of arthritis, doesnt switch on overnight. It creeps in, and dogs adapt by moving a little less, shifting weight off a sore leg, or simply resting more. To us, it looks like just getting old.

The problem is, by the time a large senior dog vocalises pain, the discomfort is often advanced. Stoicism isnt a sign that everything is fineits a sign that we need to become better at reading the little clues. Three things you can do this evening

  1. Watch how your dog rises after a long nap. Does he rock forward a few times before standing? Does he take a moment to stretch out one back leg? That stiffness, even without a limp, is a classic pain indicator.
  2. Grab your phone and film a few steps. Walk your dog on a flat, quiet surface for 10 seconds. Later, watch the video and look for a head bob (the head dips when the sore leg hits the ground) or a bunny-hopping gait with the back legs moving together instead of independently.
  3. Offer a gentle hand. Run your palm lightly over your dogs shoulders, hips, and down each leg. If he flinches, turns his head sharply, or the skin twitches under your touch, note the spotit doesnt mean you hurt him, it means the area was already tender.

Your at-home pain check: 5 signs to watch for tonight

  • Does your dog avoid hard floors? A senior who suddenly prefers carpet or rugs to tiles or floorboards is often protecting sore joints from slipping and jarring.
  • Has the wag changed? Look at the tails neutral position. A Labrador whose tail hangs low or doesnt lift as high during a greeting may have lower back or hip pain.
  • Is there phantom licking? Licking at a paw, wrist, or knee over and overwithout a visible woundis a common self-soothing behaviour for joint pain. Its not a habit; its a sign.
  • Are they suddenly fussy about touch? A dog who normally loves a belly rub but now moves away when you stroke near the hips or spine is telling you something.
  • Have everyday movements become a negotiation? Hesitation before sitting, lying down with a grunt, or refusing to lift a leg to pee (males) can point to pain that isnt making a sound.

When to call your vet right away

Some pain signals need urgent attention, no matter how stoic your dog is. Please dont wait if you see any of these red flags:

If your dog cannot put any weight on a leg, has obvious swelling or deformity after an injury, shows sudden severe pain (vocalising, flinching, hiding), or seems unable to stand at all this needs urgent vet assessment. Do not give human pain medication.

Human painkillers like ibuprofen, paracetamol, or aspirin can be fatally toxic to dogs, even in tiny doses. In an emergency, your calm presence and a quick trip to a vet or after-hours clinic are the safest help you can give.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why dont dogs show pain the way humans do?
Dogs evolved to hide weakness as a survival mechanism. In a pack, showing pain could mean losing status or becoming a target. Even our couch-loving family pets carry that instinct, which means they rarely vocalise unless pain becomes unbearable.

Can a dog be in pain but still eat and wag its tail?
Absolutely. Many senior dogs with chronic joint pain will still enjoy meals and greet you happily. Tail-wagging is a social signal, not a pain-free guarantee. The key is to look for subtle shifts in movement, resting posture, and willingness to do things they used to love.

Is it true that big dogs are more stoic than small dogs?
While every dog is different, larger breedsespecially those originally bred to work or guardtend to have a high tolerance for discomfort and a strong drive to keep going. This can mask conditions like hip dysplasia or elbow arthritis for years, making regular gentle physical checks at home especially important.

That gentle giant on your lounge has spent a lifetime giving you loyalty without complaint. Returning the favour with watchful, informed care is one of the kindest things you can offer.
