When Your Senior Dog Needs the Vet for Arthritis

July 27, 2026 · 4 min read

When It's Time to See Your Veterinarian

Occasional stiffness may improve with rest, but certain signs should never be ignored.

Schedule a veterinary examination if your dog:

  • Cries or whines because of pain
  • Refuses to bear weight on a leg
  • Develops obvious joint swelling
  • Has difficulty standing or walking
  • Stops eating or becomes unusually inactive

Your veterinarian may recommend anti-inflammatory medication, prescription pain relief, physical rehabilitation, weight management, or newer treatments designed to improve joint comfort. Every dog is different, so the best treatment plan should always be tailored to your pet's individual needs.

Growing Older Doesn't Mean Giving Up Comfort

Watching a beloved dog slow down can be heartbreaking.

The good news is that arthritis doesn't have to take away their happiness. With early recognition, gentle exercise, proper nutrition, supportive bedding, and appropriate veterinary care, many senior dogs continue to enjoy comfortable, active lives for years.

Sometimes the greatest act of love isn't asking them to keep up with us—it's slowing down and walking beside them.
