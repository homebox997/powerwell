Why Your Senior Dog May Be Hiding Pain (and How to Spot It)

August 04, 2026 · 5 min read · PawWell Editorial

The morning walk that told a different story

Yesterday, you noticed Bearyour nine-year-old Labradorlagging behind on the beach. He didnt whimper. He didnt limp dramatically. He just sat down at the high-tide mark and watched the waves instead of charging into the shallows like he used to. You put it down to age, or maybe the warm morning, and carried on.

But later, when he took three tries to heave himself onto his bed and gave a quiet sigh, something niggled. Is he just getting old or could he actually be in pain?

One of the most common myths we hear

If a dog is hurting, theyll yelp or cry. It makes sense, doesnt it? We do it. Our kids do it. But dogsparticularly our large, steady breeds like Labradors, Golden Retrievers, Rotties, and Mastiffsare wired differently. In the wild, showing pain meant weakness, and weakness made you a target. So they learned to hide it. That instinct didnt vanish when they moved onto our lounge-room rugs.

The real truth: your senior dog can be in significant discomfort and still wag their tail when you pick up the lead. Pain in large older dogs often whispers, not shouts. And learning to hear those whispers is one of the kindest things you can do.

A quiet example did you spot it?

Illustrative scenario not a real patient.

Bear, a 10-year-old Rottweiler cross, had been just slowing down for six months. His owners thought he was content to skip the evening walk. He still ate his dinner, still leaned against legs for scratches. But at his annual vet check, the vet found silently advanced arthritis in both elbows and a stiff low-back that the owners hadnt linked to pain. Because Bear never once cried out.

Heres what was actually happening: Bear was moving less to avoid discomfort. Hed stopped jumping into the car, but theyd bought a ramp and thought it was convenience. Hed become a bit grumpy when the grandkids tried to hug him around the neckagain, blamed on just getting cranky with age. All because no one told them that not showing pain is a dogs default, not a sign of comfort.

Why large breeds are especially good at hiding pain

Our big dogs carry their weight differently. A 40-kilo Labrador has enormous pressure on hips, knees, and shoulders. When those joints begin to ache, the dog doesnt screamthey offload. You might see:

  • A barely perceptible head bob when they walk.
  • Sitting down for a rest after only 10 minutes of sniffing.
  • Choosing to lie on your bed instead of the cool tiles, because getting up from soft surfaces hurts less than from hard ones.

These are pain-related adaptations, not just getting older. They are your dogs silent language. And theyre especially common in breeds prone to osteoarthritis: Golden Retrievers, Labradors, German Shepherds, Rottweilers, Bernese Mountain Dogs, and Mastiffs. If you have one of these, consider everyday stiffness a possible pain signal, not a personality quirk.

Today you can do: a simple at-home pain check

You dont need a vet degree to spot the quiet signs. Tonight, while your dog is relaxing, observe these five things. If you tick two or more, its worth a chat with your vetnot a panic, just a chat:

  • Rising movement. Does your dog hesitate, groan, or use multiple attempts to get up from a lie-down, especially on a hard floor?
  • Stairs and steps. Do they avoid the back steps, or bunny-hop with both back legs together instead of stepping alternately?
  • Resting postures. Are they lying in odd positions (like frog legs splayed tight behind) to avoid flexing a joint? Does the joint look thicker than the other side?
  • Behaviour shifts. Has a normally patient dog become space-guarder, or a once-physical dog stopped leaning against you? Avoidance of touch or a low, slow tail wag can be clues.
  • Panting or licking. Is your dog panting at night when its not hot, or persistently licking the inside of a wrist or the top of a paw? Unexplained panting can be a quiet pain signal, and licking often targets a sore spot.

Write what you see in a notebook for a week. Patterns are easier for your vet to interpret than a single odd moment. And remember, you arent diagnosingyoure simply being your dogs voice.

When to call your vet right away

While most senior dog pain is build-up over time, some situations need immediate help. Do not wait, and never give human pain medicationdrugs like ibuprofen are toxic to dogs.

If you notice any of these red-flag signs, ring your vet or an emergency service immediately:

  • Your dog cannot put any weight on a leg at all.
  • Obvious swelling or deformity after a fall, knock, or injury.
  • Sudden severe painvocalising (yelping, screaming), flinching, hiding, or trembling.
  • Seems unable to stand at all, even with gentle encouragement.

A dog in this state is in crisis, and prompt veterinary care can prevent long-term damage. Trust your gutif youre genuinely worried, make the call.

Frequently Asked Questions

Isnt slowing down just normal for an old dog?
Yes, some slowing is natural. But if your dog suddenly stops doing something they used to enjoylike fetching a ball or climbing into the uteits more likely pain than simply old age. A vet check can distinguish the two and often dramatically improve quality of life.

My dog still eats and wags their tailcan they really be in pain?
Absolutely. Appetite and a wagging tail are poor measures of pain in dogs. Many dogs with advanced arthritis still eat heartily and greet you at the door, because they push through discomfort for things they love. Look instead at movement and resting behaviour, as described in the checklist above.

Can I give my dog Panadol or aspirin for pain?
No, never. Human pain relievers can be extremely dangerous to dogs, causing stomach ulcers, liver failure, or worse. Only give medication that has been specifically prescribed by a veterinarian who has examined your dog.

Bears story reminds us: our big-hearted dogs dont complain, but that doesnt mean theyre not trying to tell us something. Youre already doing the most important thingnoticing and asking questions. From here, a gentle vet visit and a few simple adjustments at home could give your mate back the comfort they deserve. He might not say it, but youll see it in a deeper, easier sigh on his favourite mat tonight.
