Senior Dog Health FAQ

Plain-language answers for Australian dog parents caring for an older mate.

Frequently Asked Questions

These general answers support, but do not replace, advice from your veterinarian.

At what age is a dog considered senior?

Most dogs are considered senior from about seven years of age. Giant breeds may reach their senior years at five or six, while some small breeds may not be considered senior until eight to ten. Your veterinarian can assess your dog based on breed, size and health history.

How often should a senior dog see the vet?

A senior dog should generally have a veterinary check-up every six months, even when they seem well. More frequent visits may be needed for a diagnosed condition or new symptoms. Regular checks can identify changes before they become obvious at home.

What health checks should a senior dog have?

A senior health check commonly includes a physical and dental examination, weight and body-condition review, blood pressure measurement, blood tests and a urine test. Your vet may recommend imaging or condition-specific tests based on the results.

What are common signs of arthritis in older dogs?

Common signs include stiffness after rest, difficulty standing, hesitation on stairs, shorter walks, limping, reduced jumping and changes in mood. Read our senior dog arthritis guide and arrange a veterinary assessment for persistent changes.

Why is my senior dog drinking and urinating more?

Increased thirst and urination can be caused by kidney disease, diabetes, Cushing's disease, medication effects or other conditions. Measure the water intake if possible, review our kidney disease guide and arrange a vet visit promptly.

What are warning signs of heart disease in senior dogs?

Warning signs can include coughing, faster breathing while asleep, tiring easily, weakness, collapse, a swollen abdomen or blue or pale gums. Our heart disease guide explains what to monitor. Laboured breathing, collapse or blue gums require urgent veterinary care.

Should I change my older dog to senior dog food?

Not every older dog needs a food labelled senior. The right diet depends on body condition, activity, dental health and medical conditions. Ask your veterinarian before making a major change, especially if your dog has kidney, heart or digestive disease.

How much exercise does a senior dog need?

Most senior dogs benefit from regular low-impact exercise tailored to their comfort and fitness. Shorter, more frequent walks are often easier than one long outing. Stop and seek veterinary advice if exercise causes limping, breathing difficulty, collapse or prolonged soreness.

Is weight loss normal in an old dog?

Unplanned weight loss is not a normal part of ageing and should be investigated. Dental pain, kidney disease, cancer, digestive problems and other illnesses can cause weight or muscle loss. Book a vet visit if you notice a downward trend.

Can I give human pain medicine to my senior dog?

No. Human medicines such as ibuprofen and paracetamol can seriously harm dogs. Never give pain relief or change a prescribed dose without advice from a veterinarian who knows your dog's health and current medications.

How can I make my home safer for an ageing dog?

Use non-slip rugs, supportive bedding, ramps or low steps, night lighting and easy access to water and toileting areas. Keep nails and paw fur trimmed to improve grip and reduce avoidable strain.

When is a senior dog's symptom an emergency?

Seek urgent veterinary care for breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, a swollen painful abdomen, uncontrolled bleeding, repeated vomiting, inability to urinate, sudden paralysis, severe pain or blue, grey or very pale gums. If you are unsure, call your vet or nearest emergency veterinary hospital.
