Joint Care

Why Your Senior Large Dog May Be Hiding Pain And What to Check Tonight

August 12, 2026 · 5 min read · PawWell Editorial

The myth: Hed yelp if he was hurting

I often hear Aussie owners say, But he never makes a sound, so he cant be in pain. Its one of the most common beliefs I gently need to correctespecially with big dogs like Labs, Goldies, Rotties and Mastiffs. These breeds are often incredibly stoic. Theyve been wired over generations to work through discomfort without complaint. So by the time you notice a limp, the discomfort has likely been building for a while.

What pain really looks like in a senior large breed

Pain in older big dogs rarely screams. It whispers. You might notice your Golden Retriever pauses before launching himself into the back of the ute. A Bernese Mountain Dog who always bounded up the back steps now gives them a long look, then climbs one slow paw at a time. These are the moments that matter.

The core answer is peaceful and practical: your dogs pain is hidden in changes to everyday lifenot just in obvious crying or limping. Once you learn to read those quiet signals, you can catch discomfort early and give your old mate much relief.

Illustrative scenario not a real patient

Imagine a 9-year-old German Shepherd named Odie. His mum notices he still eats dinner, wags his tail and follows her from room to room. But lately, hes been getting up a bit stiffly in the morning. He used to lie flat-out on the tiles; now he curls up on his bed in one position. On their usual beach walk, he trots behind her instead of leading the way. Odie never whimpers, but his life is quietly shrinking. His owner sees it as just old age. In truth, its probably chronic joint pain.

Why dogs hide pain

Its a survival instinct. In the wild, showing weakness could attract predators. Large working breeds, in particular, can be so happy to please that they keep going even when joints ache or muscles are sore. Your Mastiff might still lumber up to greet you, tail swinging, even if his hips are uncomfortable. Thats not a sign hes pain-free; its a sign of his big, loyal heartand a sign we need to be a bit more detective-like as owners.

Look for subtle shifts in:

  • The way he rises from a sit or a down (needs a few seconds, rocks forward, uses front legs more)
  • Stair and ramp use (avoids steps, or bunny-hops up)
  • Jumpinginto the car, onto the couch (now hesitates or tries to use a paw as a bridge)
  • Licking or chewing at a particular joint, often the wrist or knee
  • Change in tail carriagelower than normal or tucked slightly
  • Behavioural signs: a little grumpier when a child leans on him, or withdrawing from pats along the back

Tonights at-home checklist Spotting hidden pain yourself

You dont need a stethoscope. Just your eyes, your hands and a few quiet minutes. Try this self-check tonight while your big fella is relaxed:

Your gentle at-home pain check

  • Rising test: Ask your dog to get up from his bed. Watch closely. Does he push up on front legs only, like a forklift? Does he pause halfway? Does he shake his body after standing as if shaking off discomfort? A smooth, quick rise is what were aiming for.
  • Morning stiffness observation: First thing in the morning, note how long he seems stiff. 1015 minutes might be normal; if it lasts longer or hes reluctant to move at all, its a sign.
  • Weight-shift at standstill: When standing on even ground, does he shift his weight to one side, hold a paw slightly off the ground, or lean forward onto the chest? This can mean hes off-loading a sore leg.
  • Touch scan: Very gently run your flat palm down his spine, over his hips, then down each leg to the knee and elbow. Pause at each joint. If he flinches, turns his head sharply, or the muscle tightens, note that spot. Dont press hardyoure just feeling for a reaction.
  • Walk enthusiasm check: Does he still bring you the lead with a sparkle in his eye, or has walkies become a heavy negotiation? Does he slow down much earlier on a flat stroll? A healthy dog, even an old one, should still enjoy a gentle rambleso a drop in joy often equals pain.
  • Sleeping position change: Dogs in pain often avoid lying on the sore side. If your normally floppy Lab now only sleeps curled in a tight ball, or wont lie on his left hip anymore, take note.
  • Breathing at rest: When lying quietly, is his breathing faster or more shallow than usual? Pain can elevate the resting respiratory rate, even without an obvious injury.

If you ticked yes to several of these, your dog is likely trying to tell you something. It doesnt mean you need to rush to emergency, but it does mean a planned vet visit and a conversation about gentle pain support is a kind next step. When to call your vet right away

Mobility red lines: If your dog cannot put any weight on a leg, has obvious swelling or deformity after an injury, shows sudden severe pain (vocalising, flinching, hiding), or seems unable to stand at all this needs urgent vet assessment. Do not give human pain medication.

These are not just a flare-up. They suggest something acutelike a torn ligament, fracture or severe infectionthat shouldnt wait.

Simple things you can start tonight

Once youve done your checklist, there are safe, practical steps to make your big dog more comfortable while you plan the vet chat:

  • Make the floor friendlier: Place non-slip mats or cheap yoga mats on hard floors and at the top and bottom of stairs. A Rottweiler who slips once on tiles can become fearful and tense, worsening joint pain.
  • Raise bowls slightly: For a tall breed like a Mastiff, bending low to eat can strain a stiff neck or shoulders. A sturdy raised feeder (elbow height) can make mealtime easier.
  • Warmth therapy: Use a microwavable wheat bag (warm, never hot) over the hips or across the back for 10 minutes while hes resting. The gentle warmth can ease morning stiffnessjust stay with him so he doesnt chew it.
  • Keep walks short and sniff-led: Move away from the 10 km beach run expectation. A 15-minute wander where he can sniff and choose the pace is often far more beneficial for a senior dogs mind and body. For more age-appropriate ideas, see our senior dog health assessment page.

Before starting any supplement or diet change: always check with your vet. Things like fish oil or joint powders can help, but they need the right dose, and they work alongsidenever instead ofproper pain management from your vet.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why doesnt my dog whine if his joints are sore?
Vocalising takes energy and goes against a dogs natural instinct to appear strong. Most chronic pain in dogs is silent. They show it through reluctance, stiffness and small behavioural changes, not noise.

Can I give my dog human painkillers like ibuprofen or paracetamol?
Never. Human medications, even in tiny doses, can cause fatal kidney or liver damage in dogs. Always get a dog-specific pain medication from your vetits safer and properly dosed for his bodyweight.

How do I tell the difference between pain and just getting old?
Ageing brings some slowing down, but pain brings a clear change in mood, willingness or comfort. If your dog still gets excited for a gentle walk and moves freely without stiffness, thats normal ageing. If he avoids things he used to love, seems flat, or shows the signs in tonights checklist, pain is the more likely culprit.

Youre not panicking; youre just paying attention. And thats what a good friend does.
