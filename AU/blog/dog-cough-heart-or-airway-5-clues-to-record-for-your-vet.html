Dog Cough: Heart or Airway? 5 Clues to Record for Your Vet

July 31, 2026 · 5 min read · PawWell Editorial

Youre drifting off to sleep when a harsh, hacking cough breaks the quiet from the dog bed. Or your big Labrador takes a drink of water, then stops and coughs like something is stuck. Its worrying and your first question is likely: Is this cough coming from the heart or the airways? The short answer is that both are possible in a senior large breed dog, but the timing, sound, triggers and other signs give you strong clues. Recording these 5 key clues at home helps your vet decide whether the heart, the windpipe, or the lungs need attention first.

A 30-Second Core Answer

A heart-related cough typically gets worse when the dog is resting or lying down, often at night, and you might also see faster breathing at rest, panting, or less energy on walks. An airway cough (like from bronchitis, laryngeal issues, or tracheal irritation) is more likely to appear after excitement, pulling on the lead, drinking, or during the day, and it may come and go without obvious breathing difficulty. Both can happen in the same dog, especially if they already have a heart murmur. Your vet will listen to the heart and lungs, and often recommend chest Xrays to see whats really going on.

Illustrative Scenario Not a Real Patient

Illustrative scenario: Monty, a 10yearold Golden Retriever, started coughing at 2am. The cough sounded soft and wet. His owner also noticed he was breathing 36 times a minute while deeply asleep most nights, and hed started lying down at the beach halfway through a walk. These together pointed the vet towards the heart, and chest Xrays confirmed early fluid buildup on the lungs (pulmonary oedema).

The One Key Point That Makes It Clearer

In largebreed senior dogs, the hearts main pumping chamber can weaken (a condition called dilated cardiomyopathy in breeds like Dobermanns and Great Danes) or the valves can thicken (like in older Labradors and Golden Retrievers). When the heart doesnt pump efficiently, blood backs up into the lungs, causing fluid thats when a wet cough appears, often worse at rest. In contrast, airway coughs come from irritation of the windpipe or bronchi, and are triggered by things like dust, exercise, pulling on the collar, or even drinking. The simple home check below helps you separate the two but never replaces a vets handson exam.

Today You Can Do: Your 5Clue Cough Log

Before your vet appointment, grab a notebook or your phone and jot down these five clues. This quick checklist takes only a minute or two each day but gives your vet a full picture.

  1. Timing. Does the cough happen mainly at night or during rest? Or is it during the day, after meals, or when excited?
  2. Sound. Is it dry and hacking (like a goose honk) or wet and bubbly (like something is rattling in the chest)? A phone recording of the cough can help.
  3. Triggers. Does drinking water, pulling on the lead, or lying down bring it on? Note if activity (walking up stairs, a short trot) starts it or makes it worse.
  4. Breathing rate while deeply asleep. Wait for a full sleep not just a doze. Count chest rises for 60 seconds (or 30 seconds, then double). Write down the number. Do this once daily at a quiet time. A rate consistently above 3035 breaths per minute in a calm, sleeping dog is a red flag.
  5. What else has changed. Jot down even small shifts: panting more at night, fidgeting and unable to settle, snoring louder, less interest in a walk, or a pot belly thats appeared quickly.

When to Call Your Vet Right Away RedFlag Emergency

Urgent warning: if your dog has trouble breathing at rest, purple or blue gums, sudden collapse or fainting, cannot lie down comfortably, or rapid swelling of the belly, stop watching at home and contact an emergency vet immediately do not delay.

Why Vets Love That Sleeping Breathing Rate Number

Your dog cant fake breaths while truly asleep. Thats why veterinary cardiologists call the sleeping respiratory rate the most reliable early warning for fluid building up in the lungs even before a cough starts. When you track it daily, a gentle rise over your dogs own baseline (say, from 20 to 30, then to 35) can give you days of extra time to see the vet before the lungs get too wet. This is especially powerful in older Labradors, Golden Retrievers, and other large breeds who can mask early heart strain simply by slowing down. If you havent started yet, tonights the night to take that first count.

What About the Cough After Water?

A cough right after drinking is often an airway sign, not the heart. It can happen because water gets near the windpipe entrance, especially in dogs with a floppy larynx or early laryngeal paralysis (common in older Labradors). That said, if youre also seeing the nighttime pattern, faster sleeping breaths, or reduced exercise tolerance, dont write it off mention it at the vet visit anyway.

A Quiet Reassurance

Watching your big companion cough in the night is unsettling it pulls you straight back to the fear that something serious is happening. The good news is that by tracking these 5 simple clues, youre giving your vet the best possible information to sort heart from airway quickly. Many heart and lung conditions in dogs can be managed well when caught early, and your notes can literally change the speed of that diagnosis. Trust your observations, jot them down, and make the call its the most caring thing you can do.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can heart disease cause a dry cough?
Yes, especially in the early stages when the enlarged heart pushes on the windpipe. But as fluid builds up, the cough typically turns wetter. Both types should be noted, because the character of the cough can change over time.

Why does my dog breathe so fast while sleeping after a beach walk?
Even a gentle walk on sand can be more exercise than it looks, and big dogs may pant to cool down. Wait until your dog is truly asleep, not just drifting off, and count then. If the rate is back to normal (under 30) after an hour of rest, its likely just postexercise recovery. If it stays up, write it down.

Is there any home gadget I can use?
Many owners find a simple stethoscope (a basic one from the chemist) helpful for timing, but your eyes and a clock or phone timer work perfectly. The key is consistency: same quiet time, same deepsleep moment, same counting method.
