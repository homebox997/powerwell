Joint Care

Your Senior Large Breed Dog Could Be Hiding Pain Heres How to Tell

August 01, 2026 · 5 min read · PawWell Editorial

If he was sore, hed whimperwouldnt he? Not always

Its a quiet Sunday morning and your old Golden Retriever, Max, is taking longer than usual to get off his bed. He still wags his tail when you pick up the lead, but hes slow on the back steps and doesnt jump into the car like he used to. You wonder if its just normal ageing. After all, he hasn't yelped or limped.

Heres the truth many Australian owners of large breeds dont realise: dogs rarely cry out in pain. In the wild, showing weakness makes them vulnerable. That instinct hasnt left our loyal companions, even in the safety of our lounge rooms. So your senior Labrador, Rottweiler, German Shepherd or Mastiff might be hurting without a single whimper. This guide will help you read the quiet signals of hidden pain in your large older dog, and give you a simple at-home checklist you can use tonight.

The core answer: pain in senior large dogs is often silent

Instead of obvious yelping, many older large breeds show discomfort through changes in movement, mood and daily routines. Stiffness after a nap, hesitation on stairs or a shorter fuse with the grandkids can all be clues. These signs are easy to miss because they creep up slowly. A young dog might bounce back from a rough beach run, but a nine-year-old Rottweiler with early arthritis may simply start lying down more often, and you might put it down to just slowing down.

Not all pain needs an emergency vet visit, but all pain deserves attention. Spotting these early hints means you can help your mate stay comfortable long before things get serious.

Illustrative scenario not a real patient

Imagine Lucy, an 8-year-old Bernese Mountain Dog from Brisbane. Her owners noticed shed stopped jumping onto the ute tray and would circle several times before lying down at night. She never made a sound of complaint. They thought it was just age. When the vet examined her, she had reduced range of motion in both hips and tight muscles along her spine. With gentle pain management and a ramp for the ute, Lucy was soon moving more freely again. No cry for helpjust subtle signs an attentive family learned to read.

Why large senior dogs hide pain, and what to look for

Pain is a protective signal, but in pack animals, showing it can mean being left behind. Even after thousands of years by our side, many dogs instinctively hide discomfort. Large breeds, often bred for stamina and stoicism, can be especially good at it. On top of that, chronic pain from conditions like osteoarthritis, common in heavier dogs, develops gradually, so they learn to shift weight or change activities to copeand we might not notice.

The signs are often what your dog stops doing, not what he starts doing. Watch for:

  • Reluctance to go up or down stairs, especially the back deck steps.
  • Taking longer to sit or lie down, or doing a crab-walk to avoid bending a joint.
  • Stiffness first thing in the morning or after a long nap that improves after a gentle walk.
  • No longer bringing you a toy for a game of fetch, or stopping halfway through a walk at the off-leash park.
  • Licking or chewing a particular joint, like a knee or hip.
  • Subtle changes in breathing: panting when not hot, or shallow breaths while resting.
  • Hesitating to get in the car or onto the couch, even if they used to love it.

These arent naughty behaviours; theyre your dogs way of saying, That hurts, so Id rather not.

Today you can do: an at-home self-check checklist

Tonight, when the house is calm, spend five minutes watching your large senior dog. You dont need special skillsjust your eyes and a gentle hand.

  • Watch him rise from his bed. Does he push up with both back legs evenly, or does he rock to one side and use his front legs more? A slow, bunny-hopping rise can mean hip discomfort.
  • Observe his walk across the room. Is there a subtle head bob or a shorter step with one back leg? Does his tail hang lower than usual? These can be quiet signs of joint pain.
  • Run your hands gently over his body. Feel along the spine, shoulders and hips (with a calm, flat palm). Does he flinch, turn his head, or tense his belly when you reach a certain spot? Compare left and right sides any warmer areas could mean inflammation.
  • Check his daily rhythm. Has he started avoiding the kitchens slippery floor? Is he panting at night when the room is cool? Does he seem grumpy when a younger dog bumps him?
  • Offer a treat just out of easy reach, so he has to stretch his neck or take a step. Does he move willingly, or does he look at it and stay put? A dog in discomfort may give up a favourite thing.

Dont force any movement that seems to bother him, and never give human pain medication Panadol, Nurofen and others can be deadly for dogs. If you notice any of these signs, jot them down and share them with your vet. You can also explore our guide to managing arthritis in senior dogs for more ideas.

When to call your vet right away

Some signs mean your dog needs urgent veterinary attention. Do not wait if you see any of the following:

If your dog cannot put any weight on a leg, has obvious swelling or deformity after an injury, shows sudden severe pain (vocalising, flinching, hiding), or seems unable to stand at all this needs urgent vet assessment. Do not give human pain medication.

Also, if your senior dogs breathing becomes laboured, gums look pale or blue, or he collapses, head to an emergency vet immediately. For non-emergency concerns, a senior dog health assessment can help you understand what to discuss with your clinic.

Frequently Asked Questions

My dog doesnt limp, so his joints cant be too bad, right?
Not necessarily. Many dogs with chronic pain in both hips or both elbows dont limp obviously because they shift weight evenly. You might only see a shorter stride or stiffness. A limp is easier to spot when just one side hurts, but bilateral pain can be hidden in plain sight.

Can I give my dog half a paracetamol to help with pain?
Never give human pain relief unless a vet has specifically prescribed it for your dog. Paracetamol, ibuprofen and other over-the-counter human drugs can cause liver failure, stomach ulcers and worse. Even a single tablet can be toxic, especially in large breeds where misjudging the dose is easy.

My old lab just seems grumpiercould that really be pain?
Yes. Irritability, snapping when touched, or withdrawing from family life are common signs of hidden discomfort. Pain lowers the threshold for coping with normal interactions. If your normally easy-going dog is suddenly avoiding pats or growling at the kids, ask your vet to check for painful areas.

A gentle note to carry with you

Noticing that your loyal friend might be in pain can be heartbreaking. But catching it early is one of the kindest things you can do as an owner of a large senior dog. Youre not failing him by seeing these signsyoure giving him a voice hes too brave to use. Speak with your trusted vet, start a pain management plan, and keep celebrating the sunset walks and Sunday morning cuddles, even if they look a little different now.
