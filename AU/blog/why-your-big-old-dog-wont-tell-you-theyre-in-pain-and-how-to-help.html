Why Your Big Old Dog Wont Tell You Theyre in Pain (and How to Help)

August 05, 2026 · 6 min read · PawWell Editorial

One of the biggest myths about pain in dogs

Lets clear up a myth that leads to needless suffering: a quiet dog is not a painfree dog. Many owners believe that if their Labrador or Rottweiler isnt whimpering, limping heavily or crying out, then everything must be fine. Thats a trap our loyal big dogs accidentally set for usbecause hiding pain is exactly what their wild ancestors had to do to survive. Unfortunately, by the time a large breed holds up a paw or refuses the back steps, the discomfort has often been building for weeks. Recognising the quiet signs early gives you the chance to help before things escalate.

The short answer every owner of a big senior dog needs

Dogsespecially large, stoic breeds like German Shepherds, Golden Retrievers and Mastiffsare masters at masking pain. They will still wag their tail, still lean into you, and may even chase a ball briefly, all while an arthritic joint or a sore spine is making them profoundly uncomfortable. The key is looking at small changes in daily habits, not waiting for a dramatic cry. Often a vet check reveals treatable pain that the owner simply didnt know was there, and early management can turn back the clock on stiffness, sleep quality and overall happiness.

An illustrative scenarionot a real patient

Imagine you live in Brisbane with a nineyearold Golden Retriever named Molly. Shes always been the first to the door for her morning walk. Lately, however, you notice she lies on her bed a bit longer when you pick up the lead. She still gets up, tail wagging, but she no longer leaps into the back of the uteshe waits for a lift. On the beach, she doesnt bound into the shallows as she used to. When you pat her lower back, she subtly flinches and looks away. She isnt crying, so you wonder if shes just slowing down with age. In reality, Molly is likely living with chronic joint pain that has crept up so gradually shes adapted her whole life around it. A vet visit reveals comfortable management options that bring back her spark.

The science behind the silence

A dogs instinct to hide weakness is deeply wired from a time when showing pain meant being targeted by a predator or losing rank in the pack. Large breeds, many of which were originally working or guarding dogs, can be especially stoic. Unlike humans, who tend to vocalise and seek help, a sore dog will simply do less. They might shift weight to another leg, lie down more, or avoid being touched in a sensitive spotall without a single whimper. The pain is still real, and can affect sleep, mood and immunity, but it speaks in whispers, not shouts. Thats why youll often hear a vet say, Hes been sore for a while, but its manageable now.

Tonights athome pain check for your big dog

You know your dog better than anyone. This short checklist helps you spot quiet signs this eveningnothing frightening, just an honest look at how theyre moving and behaving.

  • Rising after rest: After a long liedown, does your dog take several moments to stand fully? Look for a rocking horse motion, where they heave the front, then the back, rather than one fluid movement.
  • Stairs and jumping: Has your dog started to pause before the back steps, or put front paws up on the couch but leave the hind legs down? That reluctance often points to hip or knee discomfort.
  • Licking or nibbling: A dog who repeatedly licks a single wrist, shoulder or hip may be trying to soothe a sore joint underneath, even if theres no visible wound.
  • Touch sensitivity: Gently run your hand along the spine, place a palm over each hip, and then press very lightly either side of the tail base. Flinching, stiffening or turning quickly away is a quiet that hurts.
  • Changes in routine: Is your dog less excited for walks? Have they stopped bringing you a toy, or do they avoid slippery floors? These tiny withdrawals are often pains first language.

If you tick any of these boxes, it doesnt mean youre facing a crisisit means youre paying attention. Share what youve noticed with your vet, because chronic pain is often easier to manage when caught early. If your dog has arthritis, you might find our Big Dog Arthritis Care Guide helpful.

When to call your vet right away

Most pain in senior large breeds is slowonset and can be discussed at a regular appointment. But there are moments when waiting isnt safe.

If your dog cannot put any weight on a leg, has obvious swelling or deformity after an injury, shows sudden severe pain (vocalising, flinching, hiding), or seems unable to stand at allthis needs urgent vet assessment. Do not give human pain medication.

Human painkillers like ibuprofen, paracetamol or aspirin can be toxic to dogs and cause stomach ulcers, kidney failure or worse. Only ever use medication prescribed specifically for your dog.

What you can do today to help a sore big dog

You dont need to wait for a vet appointment to start gentle changes. These steps are safe and can bring comfort tonight:

  • Place a nonslip yoga mat or cheap carpet offcuts on floorboards and tiles your dog crosses often. Slipping jars sore joints.
  • Lift food and water bowls a few centimetres using a stable stand so your dog doesnt have to stoop painfully low.
  • Swap one highimpact activity (like repeated ball throwing) for a short, snifffocused walk or a shallow paddle at the beach.
  • Use a supportive harness (such as a Help Em Up or similar) for dogs who need help navigating the back steps or the ute.
  • Apply a warm (not hot) wheat bag wrapped in a towel over stiff hips or shoulders for 10 minutes while you sit together quietly.

These arent cures, but they reduce daily strain and show youre taking action while you organise professional advice.

Common ways pain shows up that owners often miss

Restlessness at nightpacing, groaning or constantly shifting positionis frequently overlooked. So is a dog who suddenly growls or snaps when a grandchild hugs them, especially around the neck or hips. Thats not a change in personality; its often a plea for the pressure to stop. Likewise, panting when its not hot, and a worried brow or tight lips, can be a dogs only way of saying Im hurting.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I give my dog a baby aspirin or any human painkiller?
Never. Human pain relief can cause severe internal bleeding, kidney damage and even death in dogs. Your vet can prescribe safe antiinflammatories and pain medication tailored precisely to your dogs weight and health.

How do I tell the difference between just getting old and genuine pain?
Ageing itself does not cause painits the conditions that often come with age (like arthritis) that hurt. If a change limits what your dog can do without distress, thats not normal ageing; its treatable discomfort. A vet can assess whether its arthritis or another cause.

My large dog still eats well and wags his tail. Could he still be in pain?
Yes, absolutely. Appetite and a wagging tail are two of the last things a stoic dog will lose. Many chronicpain patients eat normally and greet you happily while silently coping with joint or spinal aches. The earlier you spot the small signs, the more quality years you can give them.

A calm word to close

Realising your big senior dog may be hurting is hard. Please know that catching quiet pain isnt a failureits the opposite. It means youve noticed what your loyal friend couldnt say aloud, and youre giving them a voice. Work with your vet to map out a plan that might include gentle exercise, weight management, supplements or vetonly medication. Many large dogs return to longer walks, sounder sleeps and a brighter face once the pain is eased. You dont need to fear the quiet signs; you just need to listen a little differently.
