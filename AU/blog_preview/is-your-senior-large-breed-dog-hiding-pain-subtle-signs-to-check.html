Is Your Senior Large-Breed Dog Hiding Pain? Subtle Signs to Check

Written by the PawWell Editorial Team · Published 1 August 2026

Frequently Asked Questions

My older dog still wags his tail and eats wellcould he really be in pain?
Yes, absolutely. Appetite and tail-wagging aren't reliable pain gauges. Many loving dogs maintain their normal greetings and food drive while quietly coping with chronic joint or back pain. It's the smaller shifts in mobility and behaviour that tell the true story.

What's the most common sign of hidden pain in large-breed seniors?
A gradual slowness to rise, especially after resting. You'll often see a 'stiff walk' that improves after a few steps. This is so common in older Labs and German Shepherds that owners mistake it for normal ageing, but it's frequently a signal of painful osteoarthritis.

Is a dog in pain if he chases the ball but limps afterwards?
Yes. Chasing and playing release feel-good chemicals that can temporarily mask pain. The limp afterwards tells you the joint isn't coping well with the activity. It's a classic pattern that your vet can help manage with rest periods, weight control, and pain relief.

This article is for general educational purposes and doesn't replace individual veterinary advice. If you're worried about your dog's comfort or mobility, please book a visit with your regular vet. Never start or change a supplement, medication, or diet without checking with your vet first.
