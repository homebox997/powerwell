Is Your Senior Dog Hiding Toothache? What Poppy’s Bad Breath Taught Her Aussie Family

Published July 26, 2026 · 4 min read

The Hidden Toothache: Poppy’s Story

G’day, pet lovers! If you’ve got a grey-faced furry mate at home, Poppy’s story might feel all too familiar. Poppy is a sweet 11-year-old Cavalier King Charles Spaniel from right here in Australia. Her family adored her, but for years they’d noticed something odd: her breath could clear a room, and she’d stopped touching her beloved chew toys. Because she still wolfed down her dinner, they assumed her teeth were okay. Turns out, Poppy was hiding a mountain of dental pain.

Myth Busted: Will a Sore Mouth Stop Your Dog Eating?

Many Aussie pet parents believe that if their dog is eating normally, their teeth must be fine. That’s a huge myth. Poppy had multiple broken, rotten teeth, yet she never missed a meal. Vets explain it’s a survival instinct—dogs will push through the pain to eat. So don’t rely on a full food bowl as a sign of good health. Even if your little mate is still polishing off their kangaroo mince, dental disease could be quietly brewing.

Signs Your Dog Might Be in Dental Pain

Poppy’s family didn’t connect the dots at first. Here are the real-world signals to watch for in your own dog:

  • Stinky breath that doesn’t improve with dental chews
  • Avoiding hard toys or refusing to play tug-of-war (Poppy ignored toys for years)
  • Pawing at the mouth or rubbing their face on the carpet
  • Being less bouncy or withdrawing from family fun

If you notice any of these, book a check-up with your local vet—even if your dog still seems “normal” at meal times.

What Does a Heart Murmur Mean for a Senior Small Dog?

During Poppy’s visit, the vet listened carefully to her chest and picked up a heart murmur. Before you panic, know this: a murmur is just a sound, not a disease. In older small breeds like Cavaliers, it often points to a leaky valve (what vets call mitral valve disease). Poppy’s heart was still coping well, so no medication was needed yet—but regular monitoring became part of her plan. If your little mate is getting on in years, make sure your vet gives that ticker a good listen at every wellness exam.

Can a Dog with a Heart Murmur Have Dental Surgery?

Absolutely—provided everything is done safely. Poppy needed 14 rotten teeth removed, and her family was worried because of her heart. Her vet took these reassuring steps:

  • A pre-anaesthetic blood test to check her liver and kidneys were up for processing the sleepy drugs
  • A dedicated registered veterinary nurse monitored her heart rate, breathing and blood pressure throughout
  • Local pain blocks around her teeth meant she needed less overall anaesthetic, cutting the load on her heart

This careful approach meant Poppy came through with flying colours. If your dog has a murmur, don’t assume dental work is off the table—talk to your vet about a tailored safety plan.

Post-Op Tips for a Smooth Recovery

After Poppy’s extractions, her family received clear instructions that you’d find just as handy:

  • Feed only soft, moist food for at least 10 days. Soak dry kibble in warm water until it’s mush, or use a top-quality wet food. Hard bits can tear healing gums.
  • Give pain relief exactly as directed. Skipping doses might cause your dog to suffer in silence all over again.
  • Watch for warning signs like excessive bleeding, swelling, or frantic pawing at the face—and ring the clinic if you’re worried.
  • Follow-up checks were booked 2 days and 1 week post-op to make sure Poppy’s mouth was healing nicely.

Stick with these tips and you’ll give your old mate the best chance at a pain-free grin.

Why Looking After Your Dog’s Teeth Protects Their Heart

Here’s a fact that many Aussie families don’t realise: the bacteria lurking in a grotty mouth can sneak into the bloodstream and cling to heart valves. For a dog with a leaky valve like Poppy, this can cause a dangerous infection called endocarditis. That means daily tooth brushing isn’t just about fresh breath—it’s a genuine heart-protecting habit. Poppy’s family now cleans her teeth every night with a soft finger brush and doggy toothpaste (never human paste!). Even if you can only manage a few swipes, it’s better than nothing.

Regular Vet Checks: The Best Gift You Can Give Your Grey-Faced Mate

Poppy’s journey could have been caught earlier if she hadn’t missed vet visits during the busy pandemic years. When she finally went in, one appointment uncovered both her sore mouth and her heart murmur. For any senior dog—whether a tiny Chihuahua in a Brisbane flat or a sprightly Kelpie on a rural property—an annual (or six-monthly) check-up is golden. Your vet will:

  • Listen to heart and lungs
  • Peer into that toothy grin
  • Weigh them and chat about any niggles

Early finds mean less pain and more waggy-tailed years with you. Poppy now plays with her toys again, even winning tug-of-war battles, and her heart is being lovingly watched. Your furry family member deserves the same chance.

If you’ve noticed your older dog has horrid breath or is less playful, don’t wait—book that vet visit today. A happier, healthier life could be just a dental clean away.
