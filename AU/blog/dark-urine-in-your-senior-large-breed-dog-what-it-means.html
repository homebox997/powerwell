Kidney Health

Dark Urine in Your Senior Large Breed Dog: What It Means

August 03, 2026 · 6 min read · PawWell Editorial

Its 6 a.m., youre still in your pyjamas, and your big old Labrador, Banjo, shuffles out for his morning wee on the back lawn. Youre holding a mug of tea, half-asleep, when something on the grass makes you blink. The patch of urine looks darker than usualalmost the colour of cola, or maybe theres a reddish tinge youre trying not to see. Now youre wide awake, and that cup of tea suddenly feels like a bad idea.

Finding dark urine or blood in your senior large breed dogs wee is unsettling. I get it. As the owner of a nine-year-old Golden Retriever myself, Ive done the early-morning squat-stare more times than I can count. The good news? There are concrete reasons this happens, and many of them are very manageable. But it is not something to shrug off. So lets talk about what might be going on, what you can do right now, and when you need to get the car keys.

What dark or bloody urine actually means

In a nutshell, urine that looks darker than normal or has visible blood (vets call it haematuria) means theres something going on somewhere in your dogs urinary plumbingthe kidneys, ureters, bladder, urethra, or even the prostate if you have a boy. Blood doesnt belong in urine, so its presence is a signal to investigate. It could be a simple bladder infection, bladder stones rubbing the lining, a kidney issue, or rarely a problem with blood clotting. Because large breeds like Labradors, German Shepherds, and Rottweilers age, we also need to keep an eye out for more serious conditions like kidney disease or even tumours. The key point: dark or bloody urine always warrants a vet visit, but it doesnt always signal a catastrophe.

An everyday scene just for illustration

Illustrative scenario not a real patient.

Imagine a seven-year-old Bernese Mountain Dog named Maggie. Her owner noticed that over a few days, Maggie was asking to go out more often, and the urine spots left on the grass looked a bit rusty. When he took a closer look, he saw a tiny speck of fresh blood at the end of her wee. He wisely booked a vet appointment. After a urine test and a quick ultrasound, Maggie was found to have bladder stones that were irritating the bladder wall. A diet change and some medication sorted her out. No drama, just a practical fix. Thats the sort of outcome we hope for, and its often what we find.

Why does urine change colour? (just one clear reason at a time)

Lets stick to one big idea: blood changes urine colour, but not all discolouration is blood. Dehydration can make urine a concentrated dark yellow, and some medications or foods (like beetroot) can tint it red. But if youre seeing actual red, pink, or cola-coloured wee, thats most often blood. Blood enters urine when tiny vessels are irritated or disrupted. Common culprits in older large breeds include: a urinary tract infection (UTI) causing inflammation; bladder stones scraping the delicate lining; a prostate problem in intact male dogs; or, less commonly, kidney disease where the filtration units leak blood. So when your vet runs a urine sample, theyre checking for red blood cells under the microscope and also analysing concentration, infection signs, and crystals. That tiny jar of wee you collect in the backyard is pure gold for diagnosis.

An important thing to note for Aussie owners: in our hot summers, a dog that doesnt drink enough can get concentrated urine, which can irritate the bladder and make an underlying stone or infection more obvious. So a slight colour change might first appear after a long beach walk on a 35-degree day. Keep that in mind, but dont let it dismiss the need for a vet check. Thirst alone doesnt explain persistent blood.

Today you can do: an at-home self-check checklist

While youre waiting for your vet appointment, here are a few things you can quietly observe at home. No poking or prodding, just note what you see.

  • Colour and timing: Is the urine consistently dark all day, or only the first wee of the morning? Does it start normal then end with a pinkish dribble? Take a mental note (or a sneaky phone photo on the grass).
  • Straining or discomfort: Watch your dog as they squat or cock their leg. Are they taking longer than usual, pushing, or whimpering? Male dogs with prostate issues may dribble instead of a stream.
  • Frequency and accidents: Has your dog suddenly started asking to go out every hour, or had an accident indoors? That urgency is a classic UTI sign.
  • Drinking and appetite: Is your dog drinking tonnes more water than usual? That can be a kidney red flag. Conversely, a dog thats off their food and lethargic alongside dark urine adds another layer of concern.
  • Check the rest: Gently lift a lip and look at gum colourpale gums might mean anaemia from bleeding. Press a finger on the gum, it should go white and then pink again within two seconds. If it stays white or looks very pale, mention it straight away.

When to call your vet right away

Lets be very clear: if your dog stops urinating entirely, vomiting repeatedly, seems extremely weak, or has a swollen abdomen, do not waitcontact your vet or an emergency vet clinic immediately. These signs can indicate acute kidney injury which can be life-threatening within hours. Dont second-guess yourself. Id rather a hundred false alarms than one missed emergency.

Other red-flag signs that need prompt attention include: collapse, pale or blue-grey gums, inability to urinate for more than 12 hours, or a sudden, dramatic drop in energy. In large breed seniors, back pain or a hunched posture can sometimes be a hidden sign of kidney or prostate trouble, so if your dog looks uncomfortable when you gently press along the spine near the hips, add that to your vet notes.

Remember, youre not overreacting. A vet would much rather see your dog early and find a simple UTI than wait until a bladder stone causes a life-threatening blockage. Trust your gut.

Frequently Asked Questions

Could my dogs dark urine just be dehydration?
Dehydration can make urine look dark yellow or amber, but it rarely causes true red or pink blood. If you increase water availability, and the colour lightens within a day, you might be right. But if its red, or you see specks of blood, thats not just thirst. Let your vet decide.

Is blood in urine always a sign of a severe disease?
Not always. In aging large breeds, simple UTIs or bladder stones are common and very treatable. However, it can also signal kidney issues, prostate problems, or a clotting disorder. The only way to know is a vet exam, urine test, and possibly imaging. Good news: most causes are manageable when caught early.

My male dog is dripping blood after peeingwhat could it be?
In intact older male dogs, an enlarged prostate can cause bloody drips at the end of urination. It might be a prostate infection or benign enlargement. Neutered males can still get prostate tumours. Dont ignore it; your vet can check with a simple exam and ultrasound.

So there it is. Finding dark or bloody urine in your big old mate is unnerving, but youve already done the right thing by paying attention. Whether its a simple bug that needs antibiotics, some dietary tweaks for bladder health, or something that needs a closer look at the kidneys, youre on the front foot. Take a sample with you if you can (a morning catch is brilliant), bundle your dog into the car, and let your vet take it from there. Youre not alone in thisand neither is your dog.
